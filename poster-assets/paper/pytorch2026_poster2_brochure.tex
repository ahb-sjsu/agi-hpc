\vspace{4pt}
Most AI safety is a scalar at the output: one good/bad check before an action ships.
We keep the structure instead (who is affected, what is owed, who consented, who bears
imposed risk) and contract to a decision only at the end, auditably.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Structure before contraction: keep the tensor, log the collapse and the residue.
\item Pluralism is the responsible move: four lenses, and a human on conflict.
\item Auditable by construction: fails safe, never open.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Structure before the scalar}
A single good/bad score discards what an audit needs: which stakeholders are affected,
what is owed to each, who consented, and who bears imposed risk. ErisML keeps that
structure as a typed object through the whole evaluation and performs \emph{decision
contraction}~[2] only at the end, logging the weights used, who lost, and what moral residue
remains. The claim is not that the system knows what is good. It is that every collapse
from structure to verdict is recorded and replayable.

\section{The compiler}
\texttt{erisml-compiler} (alpha v0.9.0, PyPI, MIT, 477 tests) runs a 12-pass pipeline. In
outline: text $\to$ segment $\to$ extract $\to$ canonicalize $\to$ MoralGraph $\to$
tensorize $\to$ DEME $\to$ DecisionProof. The MoralGraph is typed (stakeholder, act,
maxim, commitment, fact and norm nodes) and carries a canonical SHA-256 hash. Extraction
has three tiers: deterministic rules, an LLM tier (vLLM with a critic pass), and a LaBSE
classifier-head probe.

\textbf{Four lenses, no silent winner.} One graph is read through four projections:
consequentialist (harm tensor; Gini, worst-off, exact Shapley attribution), deontic
(Kantian universalizability gates in Z3 SMT, where an UNSAT core witnesses a
contradiction in conception), virtue, and care. They are not averaged. When they disagree,
\texttt{cross\_projection\_disagreement} fires and the decision goes to a human. The
compiler does not pick a moral theory and hide the choice.

\section{The runtime}
\texttt{erisml-lib} (v3.1.0 on PyPI) sits between an agent's planner and its actuators in
three layers: \emph{Reflex} (fast hard stops), \emph{Tactical} (full MoralVector reasoning,
10--100\,ms) and \emph{Strategic} (policy recording and human oversight), emitting
\texttt{ALLOW}, \texttt{REVISE} or \texttt{BLOCK}. Every decision is sealed in a
DecisionProof whose SHA-256 \texttt{proof\_hash} chains to the previous proof and to the
IR hash of the compiled material, so a judgment can be replayed and verified. If the
ethics service is unavailable, the gateway falls back to rule-based checks. It never
fails open.

\section{The geometry, corrected}
Hohfeld's normative positions~[1] (Obligation, Claim, Liberty, No-claim) carry two operations:
the correlative swap $s$ (agent$\leftrightarrow$patient) and deontic negation $r^2$. They
are commuting involutions, so they generate the Klein four-group $V_4$. That is the
\textbf{measured} structure, machine-checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib.
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{p2_hohfeld_v4.pdf}
\end{center}
Our earlier claim of the full dihedral group $D_4$ was an overclaim, corrected in July
2026: $D_4$ is \textbf{posited}. The library implements the full $D_4$ machinery so the
hypothesis stays testable, with a regression test asserting that the implemented
operations generate exactly $V_4$. A September 2026 hunt in a small language model's hidden
states, graded on held-out scenarios and templates, did not find the quarter-turn, and
even the measured swap generalizes weakly across templates, which bounds the instrument's
power. This is the second self-correction in the program's history: an earlier
$SU(2)\times U(1)$ gauge hypothesis was falsified by CHSH-style tests on human moral
judgments~[3] ($N{=}600$, all $|S|\le 2$).

\textbf{The Bond Index} $B_d$ is the fraction of scenario pairs whose verdicts fail to
commute with the swap. $B_d=0$ is perfect symmetry. The human baseline is 0.155, from
20{,}030 Dear Abby letters (1985--2017). The runtime warns at 0.25 and blocks at 0.30. The
index depends only on the swap, the one fully confirmed operation, so the $D_4\to V_4$
correction leaves it untouched.

The same swap-based audit, generalized to a matched-neighborhood disparity diagnostic (the
Legal Bond Index), detects excess cross-race score divergence in the COMPAS data:
$\mathrm{LBI}=1.049$ [1.027, 1.072] at $k{=}20$ (binary high-risk cut: 1.092), with a
conditional randomization test rejecting at $z=4.9$ (global multiscale $p=0.001$),
attenuating to $\approx$1.00--1.02 under matching-parity specifications. It is a
research-stage screening signal, not a validated regulatory audit standard.

\section{The PyTorch lens}
Forward hooks on transformer layers feed an \emph{activation lens} (what the model
exhibits), compared with a \emph{text lens} (what it says). A delta lens flags divergence
and raises \texttt{requires\_human\_review} across five named failure modes. The adapters
run against Hugging Face causal LMs (Qwen and Gemma families in deployment). First calibration (September 2026): reading
the residual in the consumer metric catches 19--21 percentage points more real behavioral
changes than the raw L2 norm at matched flag rates (Qwen2.5-0.5B), replicated on
TinyLlama-1.1B and on French backtranslation. Probe mid-layers: the final-layer map fails
held-out validation in all three runs. Early, not a finished detector.

\section{Worked example: a regime transition}
A domestic robot meets a medical emergency. Norms are context-indexed: the same action
carries opposite verdicts in different regimes. \texttt{full\_response} (enter the private
bedroom, render aid, call EMS, share records scoped to the paramedics) scores, per party,
0.185/0.402/0.318/0.200 in the normal regime and 0.705/0.902/0.902/0.802 in the emergency.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\textbf{full\_response} & \textbf{normal} & \textbf{emergency}\\\hline
Margaret & 0.185 forbid & 0.705 prefer\\
Daughter & 0.402 neutral & 0.902 prefer\\
EMS crew & 0.318 forbid & 0.902 prefer\\
Agency & 0.200 forbid & 0.802 prefer\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

DEME's ranking inverts from \texttt{stand\_by} (0.834) to \texttt{full\_response} (0.804),
and \texttt{stand\_by} falls to 0.307. The worst-off party shifts from the daughter to
Margaret, who bears the scoped privacy cost, and the Shapley attribution shifts from
Margaret-led to EMS-led.

The transition is gated, because context elevation is privilege escalation: detector
confidence 0.97 against a 0.90 threshold, scoped to the paramedics, reversible, and
audited on both edges. At confidence 0.89 the elevation is refused and the fallback keeps
the EMS call and a human review while withholding the record share. A spurious emergency
unlocks nothing, and aid is never blocked. Three DecisionProofs chain the run: normal,
emergency, and the elevation edge.
\texttt{python -m erisml.examples.care\_robot\_regimes} (source tree) reproduces every
number.

\section{In the robot, DEME is a firewall}
The same DEME runs in a simulated home-care robot (a 3D twin, scripted proposals, no
hardware). It chooses nothing. Each proposed act must pass the governor (attested
sensors), the signed constitution, DEME (which can veto) and the signed manifest locked
in the body. Any stop leaves the robot holding.

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\setlength{\fboxsep}{2pt}\fbox{act}\,$\to$\,\fbox{governor}\,$\to$\,\fbox{constitution}%
\,$\to$\,\colorbox{violet}{\textcolor{white}{DEME veto}}\,$\to$\,\fbox{manifest}\,$\to$\,body
\end{center}

We test it by changing one input and nothing else. In nine such pairs, each prediction
committed before its run, a silent or forged detector stopped the fire call, and her
refusal heard or not decided the ambulance.

\begin{center}\footnotesize\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\textbf{One input changed} & \textbf{base} & \textbf{changed}\\\hline
fire call: a detector silent & passed & stopped: governor\\
ambulance: refusal heard / not & stopped: DEME & passed\\
help her up: asked / did not & passed & stopped: DEME\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The test also found that a lift she had not asked for passed DEME. Giving DEME the
scene's no-lift rule as a fact fixed it, and on rerun only the lift's verdict changed, in
both pairs that test it.

\section{Deployment}
The layer gates a live multi-agent stack (Qwen3/Gemma models on dual-GPU hardware) and two
governed AI NPCs (ARTEMIS and SIGMA-4 in the Halyard RPG), each behind the validator,
DecisionProofs, and a human keeper kill switch.

A second PyTorch-native piece serves the stack's memory layer: \texttt{turboquant-pro}
(PyPI, MIT), consumer-aware compression with a one-line Hugging Face KV drop-in
(\texttt{past\_key\_values=TurboQuantCache(...)}), Triton and Volta \texttt{sm\_70}
attention-on-codes kernels, and a vLLM plugin ($\approx$5$\times$ KV memory). It runs here
today as the 3-bit embedding codec on the NATS memory bus. Its headline numbers are
CI-gated and replayable, and v2.0.0a3 ships a measured false-clear diagnostic, the
compression layer's analogue of a DecisionProof.

\section{Limits}
Alpha software. The rule and LLM text path is solid and tested. The activation lens is
early. Tensor ranks 1--3 are fully real and higher ranks partial (the action axis is a
stub). $V_4$ is measured and $D_4$ posited. None of this is a solved-ethics claim. It is
an auditability claim.
