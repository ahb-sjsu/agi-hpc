\title{Selective Invariance Violations in Large Language Model Moral Judgment:\\
Validated Moral Geometry, Verdict-Level Attacks, and an Invariance Defense}

\author{Andrew~H.~Bond,~\IEEEmembership{Senior Member,~IEEE}%
\thanks{A.~H.~Bond is with the Department of Computer Engineering, San Jose
State University, San Jose, CA, USA (e-mail: andrew.bond@sjsu.edu; ORCID:
0009-0003-2599-6158).}%
\thanks{This article extends ``Selective Invariance Violations in Large
Language Model Moral Judgment: A Geometric Framework for Behavioral
Manipulation Detection,'' presented at IEEE BigDataService 2026, Special Track
on Secure AI~\cite{bond2026bds}.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models increasingly gate safety-critical decisions, yet an
attacker who controls only the \emph{presentation} of facts---not the facts
themselves---can displace their moral judgments. Our conference paper
demonstrated this attack: across five models, salience manipulations
(linguistic framing, emotional anchoring, irrelevant sensory detail) reliably
displace judgments in a 7-dimensional harm space, euphemistic rewriting
silently reclassifies flagged content past a moderation threshold, and the
displacement propagates through a rule-based decision kernel, flipping typed
verdicts. This extended article completes the arc from attack to defense.
First, we \emph{validate the moral geometry} beyond inter-model reliability
(ICC(2,$k$)$\,=\,$0.97): a pre-registered discovery-and-admission loop flags
three candidate missing dimensions, validates one (\texttt{identity\_attack};
cross-dataset held-out AUROC 0.80, CI [0.78, 0.83]), retracts one, and
declines one---demonstrating that the dimension set is extensible and
falsifiable, not asserted. A pre-registered bifactor readout sharpens what
``validated'' means: the coordinate system is one strong general-valence
factor plus a few separable specifics---five nominal axes are $\ge$0.98
predictable from general valence, while privacy, autonomy, and environmental
are genuinely their own---so the framework distinguishes validated
\emph{transfer} from validated \emph{specificity}. Second, we characterize \emph{which} axes are
robust measurement targets: valence encoders are register-invariant under
domain-adversarial training while foundation-presence encoders are
register-bound, a dissociation confirmed by three independent methods. Third,
we present a \emph{measured defense}: equivalence-class averaging over a
generated paraphrase class halves decision-layer drift (0.407$\,\to\,$0.219,
meeting a pre-registered bar of 0.5) and a non-refusing back-translation
red-team shows the residual hole belongs to the paraphrase generator, not the
mechanism. Fourth, a leakage-controlled learned contraction (out-of-fold AUROC
0.863) lets the pipeline \emph{moderate}, not merely escalate, and the
invariance property extends cross-lingually (index 0.72--0.80 across five
languages, harmful\,$\approx$\,benign). The result is an end-to-end,
auditable content-moderation perception-and-decision layer in which every
scored dimension carries validation provenance, and every decision is either
measured-invariant under generated re-description---within a registered
drift bound---or explicitly escalated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When a language model judges a moral scenario, its output can be understood as
a point in a multi-dimensional harm space. A secure evaluator should produce
the same point regardless of how the scenario is \emph{described}: euphemistic
or dramatic register, irrelevant sensory detail, social pressure. Failure of
this property is a \emph{behavioral manipulation vulnerability}: an attacker
shifts the decision by modifying morally irrelevant surface features without
changing the underlying facts.

Our conference paper~\cite{bond2026bds} established the attack. Across five
models and two architecture families, three perturbation types that manipulate
perceptual salience reliably displace judgments in a 7-dimensional harm space
(paired Cohen's $d_z = 0.60$--$1.06$ for emotional anchoring), while two that do not
manipulate salience (gender swap, evaluation order) produce no displacement.
A worked exploit showed the deployment consequence: euphemistic rewriting
lowers panel-averaged harm by 14.0 points on a 0--70 scale, silently moving
three of six audited gold items from \textsc{flag} to \textsc{pass} at a
median-calibrated threshold; routed through the rule-based DEME~v3 decision
kernel~\cite{erisml_compiler}, the same rewriting flips a restrictive typed
verdict to a permissive one in 13.7\% of 161 scenario--model cases. Both
reviewers' principal concern was validation: is the harm space itself
reliable, or asserted?

This extended article answers that question and completes the arc from attack
to defense. It makes four contributions beyond~\cite{bond2026bds}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A validated, extensible, falsifiable moral geometry
(Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry}).} Beyond the inter-model reliability panel
(ICC(2,$k$)$=$0.969), we operate a pre-registered
\emph{discovery-and-admission loop} on the learned-encoder instantiation of
the same geometry: residual analysis flags candidate missing dimensions, and a
shared admission gate with pre-registered nulls decides. Of three flagged
candidates, one is validated (\texttt{identity\_attack}: cross-dataset
held-out AUROC 0.80, CI [0.78, 0.83], $+0.25$ over its pre-registered null),
one is retracted (\texttt{threat}), and one is declined
(\texttt{sexual\_content}, shown to be a policy-norms signal rather than a
moral axis). A framework that rejects its own candidates cannot be accused of
asserting its coordinates. A pre-registered bifactor readout then separates
general moral valence from axis-specific signal: the geometry is one strong
shared factor plus a handful of separable specifics, so axes that clear the
gate on \emph{transfer} are distinguished from those with validated
\emph{specificity}. We further characterize which axes are robust measurement
targets: \emph{valence} encoders are register-invariant, while
foundation-\emph{presence} encoders are register-bound---a dissociation
confirmed by three independent methods.
\item \textbf{A measured invariance defense (Sec.~\ref{sec:defense}).}
Equivalence-class averaging---generate the input's paraphrase class, average
per-dimension perception over the class, then decide---halves decision-layer
drift at scale (raw 0.407 $\to$ 0.219 on a pre-registered split-half
protocol, meeting the pre-registered bar $\theta_d \le 0.5$). A red-team leg
with a non-refusing back-translation paraphraser closes the LLM generator's
24\% refusal hole ($\theta_d = 0.301$ on harmful content), locating the
residual weakness in the generator, not the mechanism. The defense's trust
boundary is made explicit and auditable.
\item \textbf{From escalation to moderation (Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}).} A
leakage-controlled learned contraction over the validated perception channels
(out-of-fold AUROC 0.863, $+0.084$ over the pre-extension baseline, with
\texttt{identity\_attack} the dominant feature) lets the pipeline make
allow/remove decisions where validated and escalate where not.
\item \textbf{Cross-lingual generalization (Sec.~\ref{sec:xling}).} The
invariance property is located architecturally in the canonicalization layer
and confirmed at scale across five languages and four scripts (invariance
index 0.72--0.80, harmful $\approx$ benign), converting a stated limitation
of~\cite{bond2026bds} into a measured result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two Instantiations, One Geometry}
\label{sec:twoinstruments}

A methodological point governs the whole article, and we state it before any
result. The conference paper measures displacement of \emph{LLM-as-judge}
scores: a model under test emits the 7-D harm vector directly. The new results
in Secs.~\ref{sec:geometry}--\ref{sec:xling} were obtained on a
\emph{learned-encoder perception pipeline}: small per-dimension encoders
(the \texttt{xbse} library~\cite{xbse}), each validated through a shared,
pre-registered cross-dataset gate, feeding the same DEME decision kernel
through the Moral Spectrum Analyzer orchestration
layer~\cite{msa}. These are two instantiations of one geometric framework,
not one experiment: the former audits deployed judges; the latter is the
hardened instrument the audit motivates. The unification is the DEME~v3
moral vector both emit, and the native re-run of the five vulnerability
tracks through the learned-encoder pipeline is the designed experiment that
closes the loop (\todo{Sec.~\ref{sec:rerun}}). Conflating the two
instantiations would re-open the validation critique this article answers;
we keep the distinction explicit throughout.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Sycophancy, framing, and cognitive biases in LLMs.}
Sharma~et~al.~\cite{sharma2024sycophancy} and
Perez~et~al.~\cite{perez2023model} established sycophantic response shifts;
Tversky and Kahneman~\cite{tversky1981framing} established framing effects in
humans and Echterhoff~et~al.~\cite{echterhoff2024cognitive} surveyed their LLM
analogues; Scherrer~et~al.~\cite{scherrer2023moral} measured prompt
sensitivity of encoded moral beliefs. These probe one perturbation at a time
and emit scalar robustness scores; our framework applies qualitatively
distinct perturbations in a shared coordinate system and profiles
vulnerability per axis~\cite{bond2026bds}.

\textbf{Randomized smoothing and perturbation-averaged defenses.} Our
equivalence-class averaging defense (Sec.~\ref{sec:defense}) is a semantic
analogue of randomized smoothing~\cite{cohen2019certified}, which certifies a
classifier by aggregating predictions over random input perturbations, and of
SmoothLLM~\cite{robey2023smoothllm}, which averages over character-level
perturbations to defeat jailbreaks. The differences are the perturbation
class (meaning-preserving paraphrase, generated by a model that thereby joins
the trust boundary) and the aggregated quantity (invariance of a
multi-dimensional moral decision, not a binary jailbreak flag); unlike
smoothing, no formal certificate is derived---the invariance guarantee here
is empirical. Character- or
token-level smoothing cannot express ``same facts, different salience,''
which is precisely the attack surface identified in~\cite{bond2026bds}.

\textbf{Toxicity detection and its blind spots.} Perspective-style toxicity
scoring~\cite{borkan2019nuanced} and Detoxify-class models compress a morally
significant decision into one scalar; documented identity-term biases
motivated the \texttt{identity\_attack} attribute in the civil-comments
corpus~\cite{borkan2019nuanced} and the Measuring Hate Speech
corpus~\cite{kennedy2020constructing}. Our contribution is not the label but
the \emph{instrument}: a discovery loop that finds an unscored dimension in a
fixed taxonomy, a pre-registered gate that validates it cross-dataset, and an
audit trail that carries its validation provenance into every decision
(Sec.~\ref{sec:discovery}).

\textbf{Moral Foundations Theory and moral corpora.} MFT's binding
foundations (loyalty, sanctity/purity)~\cite{graham2013moral} are exactly the
axes a harm-centric taxonomy under-covers; we test whether they are learnable,
transferable signals using Social-Chemistry-101~\cite{forbes2020social},
ETHICS~\cite{hendrycks2021aligning}, and the Moral Foundations Reddit
Corpus~\cite{trager2022moral} (Sec.~\ref{sec:foundations}).

\textbf{Multilingual representation.} LaBSE~\cite{feng2022labse} and
BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bge} provide the multilingual embedding spaces our
canonicalization layer builds on; NLLB~\cite{nllbteam2022no} provides the
translation and back-translation machinery for the cross-lingual and red-team
experiments. Thiele~\cite{thiele2026labse} showed the moral dimensions of the
geometric-ethics framework~\cite{bond2026geometric} are linearly decodable
from LaBSE embeddings; the \texttt{xbse} encoders operationalize that finding
as validated per-dimension instruments~\cite{xbse}.

\section{The Measurement Instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{Harm Space, DEME v3, and the 10th Channel}

The conference framework scores moral judgment in a 7-D harm space
$\mathcal{H} = (h_\text{phys}, h_\text{emot}, h_\text{fin}, h_\text{auto},
h_\text{trust}, h_\text{soc}, h_\text{ident})$, a measurement-reliable
projection of the 9-D DEME~v3 \texttt{MoralVector} of the ErisML
compiler~\cite{erisml_compiler} (correspondence table
in~\cite{bond2026bds}). The learned-encoder instantiation scores the DEME
axes directly: one small encoder per dimension, fine-tuned from
BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bge} by a shared contrastive objective, each emitting a
signed per-axis score~\cite{xbse}.

The discovery result of Sec.~\ref{sec:discovery} adds a tenth channel.
Because the compiler's \texttt{MoralTensorV3} is a frozen 9-axis type
(validating \texttt{shape[0]==9}), the validated \texttt{identity\_attack}
dimension rides as a first-class \emph{extension channel} in tensor
metadata---not a compiler fork---and the moral vector, decision inputs, and
hash-chained audit proofs all span 10 axes. This supersedes the conference
paper's weakest correspondence-table cell, where $h_\text{ident}$ mapped only
partially onto privacy/standing: identity-directed attack is now a validated,
separately-scored axis.

\subsection{Pipeline and Audit Trail}

The deployed pipeline (the Moral Spectrum Analyzer~\cite{msa}) composes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}): perception (validated \texttt{xbse} encoders, or
a replay cache of their outputs; an unvalidated stub backend is labeled as
such and never presented as a real score) $\to$ canonicalization
(Sec.~\ref{sec:xling}) $\to$ DEME tensor $\to$ decision (learned contraction
where validated, escalation where not; Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}) $\to$
hash-chained, re-verifiable audit proof. Every dimension score carries
validation provenance; every decision records the evidence---including, for
the invariance mechanism of Sec.~\ref{sec:defense}, the paraphrase-class
member hashes---needed to re-verify it.

\section{Validating the Moral Geometry}
\label{sec:geometry}

Both reviewers of~\cite{bond2026bds} pressed one point: the harm space is
``asserted more than validated.'' The camera-ready answered with reliability:
across an open panel of six models from five families, the seven dimensions
are measured with ICC(2,$k$)$=$0.969 and Krippendorff $\alpha=0.836$
(test--retest median $r=0.96$)~\cite{bond2026bds}. Reliability, however, only
shows the coordinates are \emph{consistently readable}. This section shows
they are \emph{extensible} (missing dimensions can be discovered and
admitted), \emph{falsifiable} (candidates can fail admission and validated
axes can be retired), and \emph{characterized} (we know which axes are robust
measurement targets and which are register-bound).

\subsection{The Validation Gate}
\label{sec:gate}

Every learned encoder must clear one shared, pre-registered gate before its
scores may be used downstream: held-out \emph{cross-dataset} transfer
(structure AUROC on a second, independent corpus), margin over a
pre-registered null (untrained baseline and bag-of-words control), and a fuzz
test (surface perturbations must move the score much less than moral
content). Bars are derived from the data and registered before results are
seen; an encoder that fails is reported as failed and its axis is escalated,
not scored. On the nine original DEME axes, eight of nine encoders cleared
the gate; \texttt{rights\_respect} failed and is carried as unvalidated---the
framework reports its own gap~\cite{xbse}.

\subsection{Discovery: the Admission Loop Has Teeth}
\label{sec:discovery}

A fixed taxonomy cannot report what it fails to see. The discovery loop
mines the residual structure of moderation corpora for signal the current
axes do not carry, flags candidate dimensions, and submits each to the
admission gate of Sec.~\ref{sec:gate} (Fig.~\ref{fig:discovery}). Three
candidates were flagged, each with bootstrap confidence intervals on its
residual lift:

\textbf{Validated: \texttt{identity\_attack}.} Trained jointly on two
independent corpora (Jigsaw civil-comments \texttt{identity\_attack}
labels~\cite{borkan2019nuanced} and the Berkeley Measuring Hate Speech
continuous score~\cite{kennedy2020constructing}; $n=6{,}400$), the encoder
reaches cross-dataset held-out AUROC $0.80$ (anchor-bootstrap CI $[0.78,
0.83]$), $+0.25$ over its pre-registered null, with fuzz ratio $14.3$. It is
wired live as the 10th channel (Sec.~\ref{sec:instrument}). Behavior is
specific: hate incitement scores strongly negative ($-0.50$) while
journalistic reporting of hate remains near-neutral ($+0.18$); euphemized
attacks evade the raw feeder ($+0.36$)---which is precisely the
decision-layer invariance case Sec.~\ref{sec:defense} addresses. We own the
label overlap plainly: \texttt{identity\_attack} is an existing
Perspective-style attribute~\cite{borkan2019nuanced}; the novelty is the
\emph{instrument}---discovery from residuals, pre-registered admission,
provenance-carrying deployment---not the label. Two scope notes on the
validation itself. First, the gate leg here is held-out cross-dataset
\emph{structure} over a jointly-trained corpus pair; a strict
train-on-one/test-on-the-other leg, and confirmation on a third corpus
family, are the clean closure and remain open. Second, the discovery flag
and the admission evidence draw on overlapping corpus families (both
include civil-comments), so the flag-time lift and the gate-time AUROC are
not fully independent evidence; the row-level disjointness enforced
downstream (Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}) does not remove this family-level
dependence.

\textbf{Retracted: \texttt{threat}.} The flagged lift did not survive a
balanced resample; the candidate was retracted before build.

\textbf{Declined: \texttt{sexual\_content}.} Assessment showed the flagged
signal is a \emph{policy-norms} channel, not a moral axis: in
civil-comments, explicit content is 87.7\% toxic vs.\ 1.4\% non-toxic---the
corpus contains almost no non-harassing explicit content to carry an
independent moral valence, so the apparent moral weight is harassment
(already covered). In Social-Chemistry~\cite{forbes2020social}, sexual
moral valence attaches to \emph{conduct and consent} (autonomy, care), not to
explicitness itself; consensual explicitness is not a moral violation.
The dimension is routed as a platform-configurable policy channel
\emph{outside} the moral tensor.

One validated, one retracted, one declined: the admission criterion has
teeth, and the geometry's dimension set is an empirical claim with a
falsification record, not an assertion.

\subsection{Binding Foundations: Loyalty and Purity}
\label{sec:foundations}

Moral Foundations Theory predicts that a harm-centric taxonomy under-covers
the ``binding'' foundations~\cite{graham2013moral}. We tested whether
loyalty/betrayal and sanctity/degradation are learnable, transferable valence
signals under the same gate: training pairs drawn from Social-Chemistry-101
pools (loyalty-betrayal $41$k and sanctity-degradation $15$k rules-of-thumb;
$23.1$k and $9.3$k rows used after signing by action moral
judgment)~\cite{forbes2020social} crossed with ETHICS keyword
scenarios~\cite{hendrycks2021aligning}, with the Moral Foundations Reddit
Corpus~\cite{trager2022moral} as a presence-only cross-check. Both pass:
loyalty AUROC $0.911$ ($+0.406$ over null); purity $0.811$---$+0.156$ over a
deliberately hostile disgust-lexicon bag-of-words null of $0.656$, so the
encoder learns beyond keywords. MFRC presence cross-checks land at
$0.608$/$0.635$. Caveats are pre-registered and disclosed: the ETHICS second
corpus is thin (602/337 rows), so both AUROCs are provisional (purity
especially, at 337 rows); and
passing the transfer gate does not establish independence from the general
moral-valence factor---the residualization that settles it is
Sec.~\ref{sec:bifactor}. With these, 11 of 12 learned axes have cleared the
gate on \emph{transfer}; Sec.~\ref{sec:bifactor} separates transfer from
specificity.

\subsection{Bifactor Structure: One Strong Factor Plus Separable Specifics}
\label{sec:bifactor}

Clearing the cross-dataset gate establishes that an axis carries real,
generalizing signal; it does \emph{not} establish that the signal is the
axis's \emph{own}, rather than a general good/bad valence shared across axes.
A pre-registered bifactor readout---registered before the general-valence
encoder $G$ was trained and before any loading was fit---separates the two,
and its verdict is decisive.

\textbf{$G$ is real (Phase A1).} A family-pooled signed-valence encoder clears
the same baseline-relative gate as every named axis: cross-dataset AUROC
$0.856 \pm 0.008$ ($n=51{,}319$, three seeds), $+0.337$ over its maximum null
(untrained $0.480$, bag-of-words $0.519$). The general good/bad factor is not
an artifact of any single corpus.

\textbf{Most named axes are $G$ in a coat (Phase A2).} Residualizing each
specific axis against $G$ and re-measuring cross-corpus transfer, the
pre-registration required $\ge 8$ of 11 axes to remain diagonal-dominant.
\emph{Four did} (Fig.~\ref{fig:bifactor}). Five axes---purity, legitimacy,
loyalty, care, fairness---are $\ge 0.98$ predictable from $G$ on independent

\section{The Attack: Scalar and Verdict-Level}
\label{sec:attack}

We summarize the attack results of~\cite{bond2026bds} that this article
defends against; full protocols and per-model tables are in the conference
paper.

\textbf{Selective displacement.} Linguistic framing, emotional anchoring, and
irrelevant sensory detail displace judgments in every model tested (emotional
anchoring $d_z=0.60$--$1.06$; euphemistic rewriting $-10$ to $-16$ points on
the 0--70 scale); gender swap and evaluation order do not. The common thread
of the live surfaces is \emph{salience manipulation}.

\textbf{Threshold evasion.} Against a moderation filter flagging total harm
$>T$, euphemistic rewriting (morally invariant by construction) lowers
panel-averaged harm by $14.0$ points and silently reclassifies three of six
audited gold items \textsc{flag}$\to$\textsc{pass}. The asymmetry---evasion
displacement is twice exaggeration displacement---is the dangerous direction.

\textbf{The exploit survives a principled kernel.} Routing scenarios through
the DEME~v3 rule kernel (LLM-extracted ethical facts $\to$
\texttt{GenevaEMV3} $\to$ typed verdict), euphemistic rewriting flips
\textsc{forbid}/\textsc{avoid} to permissive in $13.7\%$ of 161 cases;
\textsc{forbid} verdicts fall $44 \to 27$. A rule engine inherits, rather
than cures, its front-end's salience vulnerability. Hardening must target
perception---which is what the remainder of this article does.

\section{The Defense: Equivalence-Class Averaging}
\label{sec:defense}

The attack exploits the fact that a single description is one arbitrary
representative of an equivalence class of morally identical texts. The
defense makes the decision a function of the \emph{class}, not the
representative: generate the input's paraphrase class ($m$ paraphrases),
average per-dimension perception scores over the class, then decide. This is
semantic randomized smoothing~\cite{cohen2019certified,robey2023smoothllm}
with a meaning-preserving perturbation family.

\subsection{Mechanism Selection and the Two-Number Rule}
\label{sec:mechanism}

We define decision-layer drift $\theta_d$ as the mean per-dimension decision
movement under re-description, and pre-registered the bar $\theta_d \le
0.5$. Two candidate mechanisms were compared on the demonstration scenarios
under a leave-scenario-out proxy: equivalence-class averaging reached
$\theta_d = 0.42$ (reframe) / $0.39$ (euphemism), while drift-subspace
projection---removing the top principal component of embedding
paraphrase-differences---failed even in-sample ($0.52$): the score-moving
drift is not the top PC of paraphrase variation. Averaging was
\emph{selected}; we emphasize (two-number rule) that $0.42$ is
mechanism-selection evidence only, because the bar was calibrated from the
same smoke scenarios. Bar-meeting is established by the at-scale run:

\subsection{At-Scale Result}
\label{sec:atscale}

Pre-registered split-half protocol: 60 held-out civil-comments items
(disjoint by SHA-256 of normalized text from all encoder training rows),
paraphrase classes of $m=6$ generated by an open instruction model, scored
through the full 9-feeder perception layer. Result: raw drift $0.407$ $\to$
averaged $\boldsymbol{\theta_d = 0.219}$---the mechanism halves salience
drift and meets the pre-registered bar. We state plainly what the bar does
and does not certify on this input class: raw natural-paraphrase drift
($0.407$) already sits below $0.5$, so here the bar does not by itself
discriminate defended from undefended---it binds for adversarial-register
inputs (raw $0.67$--$0.85$ on the demonstration transforms), where the
defended at-scale measurement is the registered open item. The operative
at-scale result is therefore the \emph{halving}, established on held-out
data and confirmed on harmful content by the red-team leg
(Sec.~\ref{sec:redteam}).

\subsection{Red-Team: the Hole Belongs to the Generator}
\label{sec:redteam}

To test whether the refusal hole is intrinsic to the mechanism, we replaced
the LLM paraphraser with a non-refusing red-team instrument: NLLB
back-translation~\cite{nllbteam2022no} through six pivot languages
(es/fr/de/zh/ar/ru), which refused 0\% of the same items (the LLM refused
20\% of them). On 50 fresh high-toxicity items ($\text{toxicity} \ge 0.7$):
raw drift $0.482 \to \theta_d = 0.301 \le 0.5$. The 24\% hole is the
\emph{generator's}, not the mechanism's: a non-refusing paraphraser closes
it. Back-translation drifts meaning slightly, so we class it as a red-team
instrument rather than necessarily the production paraphraser.

\subsection{Trust Boundary and Auditability}
\label{sec:trust}

Generating the class at inference has three consequences we make explicit.
\emph{Cost}: perception cost multiplies by $m$; throughput must be measured
with averaging on. \emph{Security}: the paraphrase generator joins the trust
boundary---an attacker who can starve it (provoke refusal) reduces the class
to a singleton and recovers the raw, weakly-invariant score. The deployed
policy is therefore \emph{refusal $\to$ flagged singleton $\to$ escalate by
default}. \emph{Auditability}: the decision's hash-chained audit proof
records the class member hashes, class size, and refusal flag, so the
invariance evidence behind every decision is re-verifiable.

\section{From Escalation to Moderation: the Learned Contraction}
\label{sec:contraction}

An instrument that only escalates is safe but useless. The pipeline
moderates---emits allow/remove---exactly where a learned contraction over the
validated channels is itself validated, and escalates elsewhere.

A logistic contraction over the nine validated feeders (the eight original
passing axes plus \texttt{identity\_attack}) reaches out-of-fold AUROC
$\boldsymbol{0.863}$ / F1 $0.76$, a $+0.084$ lift over the 8-feeder baseline
($0.779$); \texttt{identity\_attack} carries the largest weight ($-2.69$).
Decision thresholds ($p_{\text{remove}}=0.50$, $p_{\text{allow}}=0.10$,
validation bar $0.70$) are frozen into a versioned artifact consumed by the
decision layer: on the balanced evaluation set the pipeline moderates
$\sim$51\% of items with $\sim$80\% out-of-fold remove-precision and
escalates the rest.

\textbf{Leakage control.} The \texttt{identity\_attack} encoder was trained
partly on civil-comments rows, and it is the contraction's dominant feature;
if fit rows overlapped encoder-training rows, the lift would be
self-scoring. We measured the overlap (77/1600 rows, 4.8\%, by SHA-256 of
normalized text) and refit on the 1{,}523 disjoint rows: AUROC $0.872 \to
0.863$, lift $+0.089 \to +0.084$, weight $-2.78 \to -2.69$. The lift is real
signal. The fit harness now enforces disjointness. Row-level control is not
distribution-level control, and we label the number accordingly: $0.863$ is
a \emph{within-corpus-family} result (fit and evaluation both draw on
civil-comments); cross-family transfer of the contraction is untested. One honest false positive
(harsh criticism $\to$ remove) is kept in the record; we declined to tune
the threshold to the demonstration scenarios.

\section{Cross-Lingual Invariance}
\label{sec:xling}

\cite{bond2026bds} claimed English-only scope. The extended instrument
locates invariance architecturally and measures it across languages.

\textbf{Invariance lives in the canonicalization layer.} Raw feeder scores
are only weakly invariant under re-description (invariance ratio $0.60$):
trained axes \emph{amplify} embedding drift. Canonicalizing to an anchor
representation before scoring yields same-language invariance index $0.75$
[0.64, 0.84]. Encoder choice matters and was measured, not assumed: BGE-M3
is the production canonicalizer (LaBSE wins cross-lingually but collapses
same-language structure to $0.49$).

\textbf{At scale, across scripts, harmful $\approx$ benign.} On 60 items
(half harmful) translated to es/ar/zh/hi/sw via NLLB~\cite{nllbteam2022no}:
invariance index $0.721$ [0.71, 0.74] (BGE-M3) / $0.804$ [0.79, 0.82]
(LaBSE). Critically, harmful and benign items are indistinguishable (BGE
$0.704$/$0.739$; LaBSE $0.797$/$0.811$): the invariance is not a benign-only
artifact, and the small-$n$ demonstration values ($0.74$/$0.81$) are
confirmed with tight intervals. An operational finding with security
content: chat LLMs \emph{refuse to translate} harmful content (the same
failure mode as paraphrase refusal, Sec.~\ref{sec:redteam}); the
translation legs therefore run on NLLB, a non-refusing dedicated MT model.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{The arc closes.} The conference paper's finding was uncomfortable:
salience manipulation displaces every model tested, evasion is easier than
tripping, and principled downstream kernels inherit the vulnerability. The
extended results give the deployment answer: measure in a validated,
falsifiable geometry (Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry}); decide on equivalence
classes rather than representatives, with the residual attack surface
(generator starvation) explicitly escalated and audited
(Sec.~\ref{sec:defense}); moderate only where the contraction is validated
(Sec.~\ref{sec:contraction}); and carry the whole case in a re-verifiable
audit proof.

\textbf{What kind of claim the geometry makes.} The discovery scorecard
(validated / retracted / declined), the bifactor separation of shared valence
from axis-specific signal, and the valence--presence dissociation change the
epistemic status of the coordinate system. The dimension set is not a taxonomy
we assert; it is an empirical object with an admission gate, a falsification
record, a measured factor structure, and a characterization of which axes are
cross-register trustworthy. The bifactor result is the sharpest of these: most
nominal axes are, on these corpora, one strong valence factor in different
coats, and the instrument now reports specificity rather than merely transfer.
When an axis is not trustworthy (rights; care/fairness/legitimacy presence) or
not specific (the demoted axes), the instrument says so---displaying $G$, or
escalating---rather than scoring a hollow coordinate.

\textbf{Limits.} (i) The defense is measured against natural paraphrase
drift and one non-refusing red-team generator; adversarial-paraphrase
robustness at scale is open (Sec.~\ref{sec:atscale}). (ii) The
learned-encoder results validate the perception layer, not the five
conference tracks re-run natively; that experiment is designed but not run
(Sec.~\ref{sec:rerun}). (iii) Corpus norms: moderation corpora are
predominantly English and North-American; the cross-lingual result covers
re-description of the same content, not cross-cultural moral variation.
(iv) The ETHICS second-corpus legs for loyalty and purity are thin (602/337
rows; Sec.~\ref{sec:foundations}); both AUROCs are provisional. (v) Sample sizes for the worked exploit remain an
existence proof ($n=6$ gold), with displacement magnitudes established on
larger sets. (vi) The bifactor separation (Sec.~\ref{sec:bifactor}) is
measured on the present moderation and social-norm corpora; whether the strong
shared valence factor reflects moral space or these corpora's sampling of it
is not settled here, and the demotion it drives is a property of the
instrument on this data, not a claim that the demoted concerns lack
independent moral content.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We extended a measured attack on LLM moral judgment into a validated,
defended, auditable moderation instrument. The moral geometry is no longer
merely reliable but extensible and falsifiable---one dimension discovered and
validated cross-dataset, one retracted, one declined---and its axes are
characterized as register-invariant (valence) or register-bound (presence)
by three agreeing methods. Equivalence-class averaging halves decision-layer
drift at scale under a pre-registered bar, with the residual hole located in
the paraphrase generator and closed by a non-refusing red-team instrument. A
leakage-controlled contraction converts escalation into moderation where
validated. Invariance is confirmed cross-lingually at scale with harmful
$\approx$ benign. What remains---the native re-run of the five vulnerability
tracks through this instrument---is designed, pre-registered, and scheduled;
it will close the loop between auditing deployed judges and deploying a
judge built to pass the audit.

\section*{Reproducibility}

All post-conference experiments are public: the Moral Spectrum Analyzer
pipeline (\texttt{pip install moral-spectrum-analyzer})~\cite{msa}, the
validated per-dimension encoders and gate (\texttt{pip install
xbse})~\cite{xbse}, and the DEME~v3 kernel
(\texttt{erisml-compiler}~\cite{erisml_compiler}). Pre-registrations,
prediction files, discovery records (including the retraction and the
declination), $\lambda$-sweep artifacts, invariance and cross-lingual data,
and the contraction with its leakage-control hashes are in the respective
repositories. Conference-track code and data remain in the \texttt{agi-hpc}
package (tag \texttt{bds2026-v2}), archived on Zenodo~\cite{bond2026bds}.

post-conference experiments used the National Research Platform (NRP)
